Georgia’s beauty market in 2026: what awaits beauticians from Ukraine

Prices one and a half to three times higher than in Dnipro, hundreds of competitors and new rules since March 2026. Which market segments are open, how much you’ll earn in the first year, and where to start.

Tbilisi is “a city of pharmacies and beauty salons”. Georgian women take care of themselves a lot and regularly, and since 2022 more than 100,000 relocants have arrived in the city – half of them Russian-speaking women looking for “their own” beautician. Beauty prices in lari are one and a half to three times higher than Dnipro prices in hryvnias, while materials cost the same. That’s what the whole economics of moving rests on.

Below: what changed in 2026, what services really cost, which market segments are open to a beautician from Ukraine and which are closed, and what the first six months look like in numbers. The part on brows and make-up comes from our wintering guide. Nails, hair, facials, laser and injectables are covered here for the first time.

Manicure and pedicure

30–60 ₾ manicure40–80 ₾ pedicure100–180 ₾ extensions

The most frequent visit: gel polish lasts two to three weeks, and clients come back more often than to a brow artist. The barrier to entry is low – a lamp, an e-file, a steriliser. Competition is high, but beauticians from Ukraine have a strong e-file manicure school, and relocated clients are used to exactly that and look for “their own” nail tech. For comparison, a nail tech in Dnipro earns about 25,000 hryvnias a month on average.

Verdict: the best segment to start with – frequent visits, quick client base, minimal investment.

Hair: colouring and haircuts

~35 ₾ haircut70–80 ₾ cut and style50–57 ₾ colour~150 ₾ balayage

Haircuts are refreshed every 6–8 weeks, colour less often but with a higher ticket. You need a station with a wash basin, so a coworking chair doesn’t always work – look for a chair to rent in a salon. Complex colouring is inexpensive in Tbilisi by European standards, so a strong colourist has room on price here.

Verdict: go for it if you’re a colourist with a portfolio; simple haircuts are a crowded market.

Facials without injections

100–170 ₾ cleansing~150 ₾ peel

Cleansing, skincare programmes, facial massage – anything that doesn’t break the skin. You need a separate room and a treatment bed, so the start is more expensive than for a brow artist. Pairs well with brows and lashes: one client, two tickets.

Verdict: a strong second service; as your only one – a slow start.

Injectables and device-based aesthetics

~99 ₾ biorevitalisation300–600 ₾ filler~550 ₾ botulinum toxin

The highest tickets on the market, but this is medical practice. Under Georgia’s Law on Medical Practice, only a doctor with a Georgian state certificate may practise independently. A beautician without a medical degree can’t inject at all, whatever courses she has taken.

If you’re a doctor from Ukraine, there is a path, but it’s long. Georgia recognises Ukrainian medical degrees: Ukraine is on the list of countries whose diplomas are valid here. But the diploma alone only qualifies you as a junior doctor under someone else’s supervision. For independent practice you need a certificate: a Ukrainian specialist certificate is either recognised without an exam if the country is on a separate Health Ministry list, or you’ll have to pass the state exam. On top of that, the law requires Georgian and, since March 2026, a work permit.

If you “inject at home” and a client gets ill, that’s Article 246 of the Criminal Code – illegal medical practice, up to 3 years, more with aggravating circumstances. Complaints are investigated by the Agency for Regulation of Medical Activities.

Verdict: doctors should start with a clinic job and get certified in parallel; beauticians without a medical degree – stay away.

Lashes and lash artistry

100–250 ₾ extensions×2.5–3 vs. Dnipro

The biggest price gap with Ukraine. Refills every 3–4 weeks, almost no equipment needed. The niche is crowded, and you win on retention and neatness, not price: undercutting gets you nothing here.

Verdict: go for it, especially combined with brows.

Laser, massage and everything “on the treatment bed”

20 ₾ laser: lip48–60 ₾ laser: face20–40 ₾ massage at the baths

Laser hair removal hinges on a machine costing tens of thousands of dollars – it’s salaried work at a clinic, not your own room. Massage is a different story: in Tbilisi a “massage parlour” sign often covers for sex services, and police raids target exactly those addresses. If you’re a massage therapist, work only at a clinic, spa or salon with a clear reputation, and name the service precisely: therapeutic, sports, lymphatic drainage.

Verdict: salaried only, and only under someone else’s reputable sign.

Wintering and relocating are two different modes

In winter you can’t legally see clients in Tbilisi. Registering as a sole trader and getting a permit for 3–5 months makes no sense, and working “from the flat for cash” breaches your lease, gives neighbours a reason to call the patrol, and leaves a mark with the migration service that will get your residence permit refused.

What you can do in winter: online consultations and training for clients in Ukraine, paid to your Ukrainian sole-trader account, content shoots, scouting coworking spaces and photographers, courses. Expenses – $1,100–1,300 a month, online income – $100–300. Wintering is scouting, not earning.

If you decide to stay: the order of steps

  1. 2 months before departure

    Profile and chats

    Bio and highlights in three languages, Tbilisi geotag, 20–30 before/after shots, booking via Dikidi, 5–6 chats of Ukrainians in Tbilisi. Cost – zero.

  2. Wintering, November – March

    Scouting

    Rent an hour at 2–3 coworking spaces in Vera and Saburtalo, check the light and sterilisation. Book yourself in as a client with two competitors. Meet an accountant. Content, models in exchange for reviews, arrangements with photographers. Expenses – $1,100–1,300 a month.

  3. Month 1 after “I’m staying”

    Documents

    Public Service Hall (sole trader, 1 day) → sole-trader bank account → “small business” status on rs.ge (1% of turnover) → work permit via labourmigration.moh.gov.ge with a video interview → residence permit. While you wait – not a single paying client. $300–600 for paperwork.

  4. Month 2

    First clients

    Only after the permit. “−20% on the first visit”, ads targeted within 3 km. Goal – 25 Google Maps reviews and 40 clients in your database. Income – $300–600.

  5. Month 3

    Price up to market

    A coworking membership, prices up to the market average, a second service. The “we’re staying” test: 60+ clients, half of bookings repeat. Income – $800–1,300.

The first months – coworking only: a make-up chair is about $3 an hour, a treatment bed $4–6, a membership $150–260. Your own 20–30 m² studio costs from $800–1,200 and isn’t needed before the second half of the year.

How beauticians actually find their first clients

Not through an Instagram with 200 followers, but through the same chats. In a 15,000-member group of Ukrainians in Georgia, beauticians advertise every two to three days: a make-up artist posts the same ad four times a week, brow artists and nail techs look for “practice models”, rent a station in someone else’s salon, recruit students for courses. You can also see demand there that doesn’t exist on Instagram: people look for Ukrainian cosmetologists, paediatricians and speech therapists, saying “Georgians won’t manage”. It’s both proof that there are many beauticians and a ready-made tactic: first bookings come from a chat post and models paying for materials, not paid ads.

Five mistakes that cost the mostSeeing clients before you have a work permit. Undercutting below $9–10 for brows – enter only via “−20% on the first visit”. Renting a studio in the first six months. Relying only on Ukrainian clients – relocants leave, Georgians stay. Starting with permanent make-up: it’s the highest ticket and the fastest way to get a scathing review in a 15,000-member chat.
About your photosA beautician’s portfolio means dozens of public photos of other people’s faces and your own, and your work number in every chat. Don’t publish clients’ faces without written consent, and keep your work profile separate from your personal one.
