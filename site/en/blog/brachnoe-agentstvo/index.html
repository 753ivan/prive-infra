Working with a marriage agency: where will your photos be a year from now?

A profile with good photos is a marriage agency’s main asset, so deleting it after you leave doesn’t pay. We look at where the photos go, where the law sides with the agency, and the three clauses to get on paper before your first shoot.

Marriage agencies that introduce Ukrainian women to foreign men make their money not on weddings but on correspondence: the man pays for letters, chats and video calls, and the agency takes a cut. The main asset in this business is a profile with good photos. That is why an agency gladly pays for your photo shoot and is reluctant to delete your profile when you leave.

If the terms aren’t written down, a year from now your photos could be on a dozen sites, under someone else’s name, and even on a “scammer” blacklist read by foreign men. Below: how it happens, why the law here tends to favour the agency, and the three clauses you need on paper.

One photo’s journey over a year

This isn’t one woman’s story but a typical scenario pieced together from investigations and accounts of people in the industry. Yours may differ – but every link in this chain is documented.

  1. Month 0

    A free photo shoot

    A scout or “manager” offers a shoot and a profile. The founder of the Kyiv agency Grace Family told the Kyiv Independent that scouts recruit women for photo shoots, and those photos may later be resold between agencies.

  2. Month 1

    The profile spreads across sites

    Small Ukrainian sites supply “verified” profiles of real women to large international portals and get a share of what men pay. One profile ends up on several platforms at once – ones you may not even know about.

  3. Months 1–6

    Others write in your name

    Letters and chats are often handled by translators or operators. Your face earns money even when you’re offline.

  4. Month 6

    You leave – the profile stays

    If the contract sets no deadline for removal, the agency is better off keeping the profile: it keeps earning. The partner site doesn’t even know you’ve left.

  5. Months 6–12

    The photos take on a life of their own

    The photos are sold to another agency or picked up by scammers. In the Kyiv Independent investigation, dozens of women’s photos were reused across several profiles under different names – often presented as “Russian women”. A Ukrainian blogger whose photos turned up in one such profile told reporters she isn’t registered on any dating site.

  6. Month 12

    Your face on a blacklist

    Foreign men keep public databases of “scammers” with photos, and face search finds them within a minute. Profiles from the Kyiv Independent investigation were already on such a list. To anyone looking you up, it looks as if you are the scammer.

Why the law won’t protect you on its own

The general rule

Your consent is required

A person’s photo may be publicly shown, reproduced and distributed only with their consent (Art. 308(1), Civil Code of Ukraine). And under the personal data law you can withdraw consent, ask which third parties your data was passed to, and get an answer within 30 days (Art. 8).
The exception that hits models

You posed for payment

If you posed for payment, your photos can be shown and distributed without your consent (Art. 308(2)). You can stop it only by compensating the author or another party for their losses. A shoot paid for by the agency, or a percentage of the correspondence, gives the agency an excuse to say: “you posed for pay – we’ll delete it if you compensate us.”

There’s a second problem: copyright in the photos usually belongs to the photographer or the agency, not to you. So a “this is my photo” complaint to a site may not work. What does work is your image and your personal data – plus whatever you wrote into the contract in advance. A good contract closes the Art. 308(2) exception in a single sentence.

Three things to get in writing

Before the shoot, not after. A verbal “of course we’ll delete everything” is worth nothing: within six months the manager, the agency or its name may change.

1

Removal when you leave – with a deadline and no compensation

A deadline in days, removal from all sites including partner sites, and written confirmation with links. And a separate line saying that withdrawing consent doesn’t oblige you to compensate anything: that is your protection against Art. 308(2).

Within 10 calendar days of the termination of this contract or the Model’s written withdrawal of consent, the Agency shall remove the Model’s profiles from all sites and send the Model confirmation with links to the removed pages. The Model may withdraw consent to the use of her image at any time; withdrawal of consent does not oblige the Model to compensate any losses.
2

A closed list of sites

A list of platforms with addresses, attached to the contract. Any new site only with your separate written consent. That way you know exactly where to check, and you have grounds to demand removal from everywhere else.

The Agency shall post the Model’s photos and videos only on the sites listed in Annex 1, stating the profile address on each site. Posting on any other site is permitted only with the Model’s separate written consent.
3

A ban on passing photos on

Not to other agencies, not to “partners”, not into profiles under another name, not into advertising. This is how photos most often slip out of control.

The Agency shall not transfer, sell or provide the Model’s photos, videos or personal data to third parties, including other agencies and sites not listed in Annex 1, and shall not use them in profiles under another name or in advertising.

Five more clauses, if the agency agrees

Who writes in your nameIf translators handle the correspondence, the contract should say so plainly. Once you leave, correspondence in your name stops the same day.
Term of consentConsent for 6 or 12 months, not “indefinitely”. Not renewed in writing – the profiles are deleted.
Copies of every photoYou receive all the photos and videos being used. Without them you won’t know what to search for.
PenaltyA fixed sum for every profile not removed on time or passed to third parties. Without a penalty, the removal clause is easy to ignore.
A real party to the contractA registered sole trader or company with its EDRPOU code, address and signature – not an “admin” on Telegram.You can check the company in open registers by its code.

Red flags

1
“You don’t need a contract”or “it’s a standard form, it can’t be changed”.
2
They won’t name the sites“We work with partners” – with no list and no addresses.
3
“The photos stay in the agency’s archive”forever, “for the portfolio” or “just in case”.
4
A “translator” or “chat operator” job adWork.ua has noted that marriage agencies disguise themselves exactly this way.
5
Removal “for compensation”If they want money for the shoot when you leave early, they are counting on Art. 308(2).
6
A passport copy with no explanation“For verification” – without saying who keeps it and for how long.

If you’ve already left and the profiles are still up

1. Find every copySearch by face (PimEyes, Lenso) and by the name used in the profile. Save screenshots with the page address and date – this is your evidence log.
2. A written demand to the agencyWithdraw consent to the use of your image and the processing of your personal data, demand that the profiles be deleted, and ask which third parties your data was passed to. Under Art. 8 of the personal data law, the answer is due within 30 days. Send it so there’s a trail: an email plus registered mail with a list of contents.
3. Go to the sites directlyMost portals have a removal form or a privacy contact. If the site operates in the EU, cite the right to erasure (Art. 17 GDPR) and withdrawal of consent (Art. 7 GDPR).
4. If they refuseA complaint to the Ukrainian Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights or a lawsuit. If your photo is being used in a scam profile under another name, file a report with the cyber police.
