Five schemes the women describe themselves

01

Face search → name → “pay up or I’ll send it to your family”

profile photo→face search→old profile with a name→family and work→demand for money

Face-search services find every page that has your photo: a profile, an old social media account, a school album. One member found her school photos this way, ones she had long forgotten. In the chat people name these services outright and write that a whole industry has grown up around them: a woman is “looked up” by photo, her relatives are found, and money is demanded for silence.

check yourself through face search before someone else does, and separate your face from your real name.
02

A “manager” or “agency” with a copy of your passport

passport “for paperwork”→selfie with your ID→your mom’s number “just in case”→demand “not to leak”

A passport, a selfie with your ID, an address, relatives’ numbers — all of it goes to a person you’ve never seen. Women describe cases where “managers” looked up newcomers themselves and then demanded money not to “leak” anything. Out of fear of this kind of blackmail, some say they don’t even take out loans: they’re afraid their passport details will be misused.

documents go only to government authorities and to the site itself through its official form. How to vet a “manager” — in our checklist.
03

A leak from a site or after “verification”

selfie with a sign→video for the site→the database reaches intermediaries→blackmail

Ad sites require proof that photos are real: a selfie with a sign, a video, sometimes an ID. Over three years, questions about verification nearly doubled, and at the same time ads appeared offering “I’ll pass verification for you” and “selling a ready-made profile”. So your verification photos are seen by more than just a moderator. Women also write about sites that spend years collecting and publishing photos sent to them — no one can get anything removed from there.

minimum data for verification, never through an intermediary, and never a photo that makes you easy to recognise outside the site.
04

Other people’s profiles with your photos

stolen pictures→fake profile→“prove to your family it’s not you”

“I found my photos on a site and called — curious who’s working under them.” Fake profiles are made from stolen pictures, and then the same profiles are used for blackmail: “prove to your family it’s not you.” In the same chat, “fake profiles for your city” are sold openly — other people’s photos are a business of their own.

if the photo is yours, you hold the copyright, and the profile can be taken down by law: here’s how.
05

A former client or partner

pictures “just for us”→a fight or a break-up→a threat to send them

The oldest scenario: pictures taken “just for us”, or messages that resurface after a fight. Here the blackmailer knows you personally and knows where to press.

don’t pay, save everything and follow the 24-hour plan.

What the law says

In all five schemes the blackmailer counts on one thing: that you’re ashamed and will stay silent. But blackmail is a criminal offence in both Ukraine and Georgia. It doesn’t matter what you do for a living: the criminal is the one making threats.

UkraineArt. 189 and 182 of the Criminal Code

Extortion — up to 5 years, up to 12 with aggravating circumstances. Disseminating information about private life without consent is a separate offence.

GeorgiaArt. 157 of the Criminal Code

Illegally obtaining, storing and disseminating information about private life, including threatening to disseminate it — 4 to 7 years.

If you’re being blackmailed right now

  1. Step 1

    Don’t pay

    That’s the main advice from everyone who helps blackmail victims. Paying doesn’t buy silence: the blackmailer learns that you pay and demands more. If you’ve already paid, stop and contact your bank or payment service immediately: sometimes the transfer can still be reversed.

  2. Step 2

    Don’t argue or plead

    Any reply, even “I won’t pay”, shows you’re reading and you’re scared. Stop replying. Block only after Step 3.

  3. Step 3

    Save everything

    Screenshots of the conversation with dates, the username and numeric account ID, phone number, payment details, links to posts. Don’t delete anything: it’s evidence.

  4. Step 4

    Lock down what you can

    Make your profiles private and temporarily hide your friends and followers lists: that’s exactly where a blackmailer looks for whom to send things to. Don’t delete accounts — just hide them.

  5. Step 5

    If photos are already out, remove them step by step

    Notices to platforms, StopNCII, Google’s form — in our guide “Your photos were leaked: what to do in the first 24 hours”; the legal grounds — in “Removal by law”.

  6. Step 6

    Report

    In Ukraine — a report to the police or cyber police about extortion. In Georgia — 112 or a police station. Women in the chat give each other one more tip: if the blackmailer threatens to “report” you, a counter-report for extortion usually cools him down fast.
