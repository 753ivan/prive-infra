Your photos were leaked: what to do in the first 24 hours

A step-by-step plan if your photos have been published or someone threatens to publish them: how to secure evidence, which laws have been broken, where to send notices and how to file a report in Ukraine and Georgia.

You’ve been sent a screenshot with your photo and a demand: “pay up, or I’ll send it to everyone.” Or a friend has sent you a link to a channel where your pictures are posted. Legally, at that moment at least one crime has already been committed, possibly two: extortion and unlawful dissemination of information about private life. What you do in the first 24 hours determines whether both offences can be proven, the spread stopped, and copies prevented.

Below is an hour-by-hour plan. Each step is based on current criminal and civil law in Ukraine and Georgia and on official platform rules; sources are at the end.

0–1 hSecure the evidence
1–3 hBlock re-uploads
3–6 hSend notices to platforms
6–24 hFile a criminal report
The key ruleDon’t pay. Meeting an extortionist’s demand doesn’t end the crime – it confirms the pressure works: the first transfer is almost always followed by a second. Don’t threaten back and don’t negotiate – anything you write can be used against you.

What the law says

Sending an intimate photo to someone you trusted is not an offence. The offence is using that photo without your consent: demanding money under threat of publication, and the publication itself. In both countries these are criminal acts.

Ukraine

Criminal and Civil Codes

Art. 189 CC
Extortion: demanding property under threat of disclosing information the victim wishes to keep secret.Part 1 – restriction or deprivation of liberty for up to 5 years; aggravated forms – up to 12 years
Art. 182 CC
Violation of privacy: unlawful collection, storage, use or dissemination of confidential information about a person without their consent.Part 1 – a criminal misdemeanour; with significant harm – 3 to 5 years’ imprisonment
Art. 308 Civil Code
A person’s photograph may be publicly displayed, reproduced and distributed only with their consent. Grounds for a civil claim for removal and compensation for moral damage.
Georgia

Criminal Code

Art. 157 of the Criminal Code
Unlawful obtaining, storage, use and dissemination of information about personal life.Prosecutors charge such cases with sentences of 4 to 7 years’ imprisonment
Art. 157¹ CC
Unlawful dissemination of information about personal life, including via social media; in prosecution practice combined with articles on coercion and stalking.up to 7 years’ imprisonment
Art. 151 CC
Threats – charged additionally if you were threatened with violence or other harm.

This summary is a guide, not a substitute for legal advice: the final charges are set by investigators, and sentences depend on the part of the article and the circumstances of the case.

  1. Hour 0–1

    Secure the evidence, delete nothing

    Investigators need to prove three things: who made the demand, what exactly they demanded, and where it was published. Collect the materials so each element can be verified.

    whoIdentifiersUsername, profile link, phone number, and if visible, the numeric account ID: a username can be changed, an ID can’t.
    whatThe full conversationScreenshots showing the date and time of each message, uncropped. Plus a screen recording: it shows the conversation wasn’t edited.
    whereLinks to the postsTelegram – in the form t.me/channel/number (long-press → “Copy link”). Websites – the full address from the browser bar.
    what it looks likeA screenshot of the postWith the page address and date visible. Intimate areas can be covered – Google’s forms explicitly allow it.
    moneyPayment detailsThe card, wallet or phone number they want the transfer sent to: police use it to identify the recipient.

    Send the archive to your email or cloud straight away: the email’s date confirms when the materials were collected. Don’t block the extortionist until you’ve saved everything, and don’t delete the conversation – without it your report loses its main evidence.

  2. Hour 1–3

    Block re-uploads: StopNCII

    StopNCII.org is a free service run by the UK’s Revenge Porn Helpline. It creates a digital fingerprint (hash) of the image right on your device; the image itself is never uploaded. Partner platforms receive the fingerprint and check every new upload against it. Partners include Facebook, Instagram, Threads, TikTok, Reddit, Bumble, OnlyFans and Google Search.

    Limitations: the service only works for images you have on your device, only on partner platforms, and doesn’t cover encrypted chats. Telegram is not a partner. If you’re under 18, use Take It Down – the equivalent for minors.

  3. Hour 3–6

    Send notices to platforms

    PlatformHow to reportLegal basis and timeline
    TelegramIn the app: “⋯” → “Report” on the channel and on each message. In writing – abuse@telegram.org with a list of t.me linksOnly public content is reviewed: channels, groups, bots. Write in English: “non-consensual intimate images”, list of links
    GoogleForm to remove intimate images from SearchGoogle aims to review requests within 48 hours and, on request, filters future copies; this also applies to deepfakes. Removes from results, but not from the source site
    Instagram, FacebookReport the post – the option for nudity and intimate images without consent; a separate Instagram formMeta itself offers StopNCII when you file a report
    US platformsThe platform’s form; if it doesn’t act – a complaint to the FTCUnder the Take It Down Act, since May 2026 platforms must remove such images and their copies within 48 hours
    Websites and hostsAbuse form or copyright infringement notice; the site’s hosting providerIf you took the photo yourself, you’re its author and copyright law applies – how it works
  4. Hour 6–24

    File a criminal report

    Ukraine. A report to the National Police (102) or the cyber police. Cite both offences: the demand for money – Art. 189 CC; dissemination or threat of dissemination – Art. 182 CC. The details are entered into the Unified Register of Pre-trial Investigations; ask for an extract with the case number. Such cases do lead to arrests: in 2026, police detained a man who blackmailed a singer with intimate videos – charged under Art. 189, up to 12 years.

    Georgia. 112 or the nearest police station; you can also go to the prosecutor’s office. Unlawful storage and dissemination of intimate materials is charged under Art. 157 CC – up to 7 years’ imprisonment, and prosecutors make arrests even for threats to disseminate. The NGOs Sapari and GYLA help draft a report for free.

    Bring a printout or a USB stick with all the materials from the first hour. Police can request data from platforms and banks – you don’t have that power.

What not to do

Don’t payand don’t “negotiate a discount”: it doesn’t stop the demands.
Don’t post in the channelwith the publication and don’t ask followers to report it – it boosts the post and spawns copies.
Don’t deletethe conversation or your account before saving the evidence.
Don’t hire “cleaners”from the comments: “I’ll delete everything for $200” in DMs are scammers too.
Don’t blame yourself.The crime is the blackmail and the dissemination, not you.
A week laterCheck whether copies have appeared: face search, username search, aggregator channels. Leaked materials rarely stay in one place – they get reposted. Add every new link to the case file and repeat the reports.
